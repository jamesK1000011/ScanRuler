# Circuit boards

A KiCad board brought into Reverse Engineer, a box made round it, and its files for the board house. KiCad's own `kicad-cli` does the KiCad work on the person's computer, through the agent's server; reading a board needs no KiCad.

1. `pcb.tools` says whether kicad-cli is found and which commands its `--help` shows. KiCad 8 or later does everything; KiCad 7 has no DRC and no CSV parts list. Not found: the person installs KiCad, or sets `SCANRULER_KICAD_CLI` to where kicad-cli is and restarts the agent's server.
2. `pcb.open { path }` reads the `.kicad_pcb`: outline, thickness, mounting holes, and the connectors at the edge — each with the wall it faces (`+x` right, `-x` left, `+y` back, `-y` front) and the opening it needs. Openings for USB-C, micro- and mini-USB, USB-A, barrel jacks, RJ45, pin headers and JST housings are typical sizes, said so; check them against the part's datasheet.
3. `pcb.import` exports the board as STEP and brings it in: the board a green body named after its file, each part a body, edge connectors named `board J1`. It measures how far the parts reach above and below the board, and each connector's 3D box. `components: false` brings the bare board. A part with no 3D model — none named in its footprint, or one kicad-cli could not find — gets a grey placeholder box named `board R1 (placeholder)`: its footprint's courtyard (else fab outline, else pads), as tall as a Height property or `_H<n>mm` in the footprint's name says, else 3 mm. The answer lists them in `placeholders` with where each size came from; the measurement, the enclosure and any clearance check see the boxes, not the real parts — add the models in KiCad for a true fit.
4. `pcb.plan` shows the enclosure before it is made: the board as the enclosure takes it, each standoff (screw size from the hole: M2, M2.5, M3, M4; heat-set insert hole or self-tapping pilot) and each opening. `pcb.enclosure` makes it with Generate's `board_box`, standing round the board where it is, with the lid beside it, top down. Settings go through `pcb.set { enclosure: { standoff, headroom, gap, wall, inserts, cutouts, margin, … } }`. The standoff holes, the gap to the board, the lid clearance and the opening margin follow the printer's fit calibration from Fits unless set (the answer's `printerFit` says which; `printerFit: false` builds without it); don't copy the calibration into them by hand. `skip: ["J3"]` leaves a connector without an opening, and `above` and `below` override what the import measured.
5. `pcb.fab` gives the fabrication package as a zip. It holds the Gerbers, the Excellon drill files (plated and unplated apart, mm), the placement CSV, the parts list from the schematic beside the board, and the DRC report. `naming: "fab"` (the default) uses Protel extensions and JLCPCB's column names, which JLCPCB and PCBWay read. The job folder also keeps a zip of the Gerbers and drill files alone, ready to upload.
6. `pcb.drc` runs KiCad's design rule check: violations and unconnected items by severity, the first 50 with their places in KiCad's coordinates.
7. Ordering it: a PCB order setup for the board's body (the **pcb-order** guide) — `pcbo_check` holds the board (`pcb.open`'s `stats` and `rules`) against the fab, `pcbo_package` gathers the order. The enclosure goes in a print setup of its own.

`pcb.stop` stops what kicad-cli is doing (or the enclosure being built). `pcb.close` puts the board away; its bodies stay in Reverse Engineer.

Electronic parts' 3D models: `catalog_models_search { query: "SOT-23", sources: ["kicad"] }` searches KiCad's own library (kicad-packages3D, CC-BY-SA 4.0 with the KiCad libraries exception). Use KiCad's package names: `SOT-23`, `USB_C_Receptacle`, `PinHeader_1x04_P2.54mm`. `catalog_models_take` brings the STEP in with its licence recorded. The Catalog panel opens it as a body.

Coordinates are in mm, centred on the board outline's box, y up, with the board's bottom face at z = 0. KiCad's own coordinates run y down from the sheet's corner; `origin` in the board's read gives that centre in them.
